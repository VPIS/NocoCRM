# The demo dataset

Read this before changing the demo dataset, its content files, its mail, its import and removal, or the demo banner.

The demo dataset is a product feature (`server/providers/crm/demo.ts`, imported and removed from the settings page), not a script or a seed. The settings' Demo data tab (`/settings/demo-data`, `client/pages/settings/demo-data.tsx`) reads the dataset's state from its query and tells the outcome in a toast, since importing or removing it changes the importer's roles and the route guards mount the page anew while the new permissions load; the outcome is written at once into that query and into the workspace's `demoPresent`, which the banner reads, and an import whose request a gateway cuts off (a 502, 503 or 504, or a lost connection, as a hosted deployment's proxy gives up before the server finishes) is not taken for a failure: the card asks how the dataset stands every three seconds, for up to three minutes, until it is there. It comes in Chinese and English, chosen on import (`language` on `POST /demoData/import`, recorded in the manifest): the same story, told with people and companies of that language: its text and everything derived from a name (the demo accounts, each language's own, looked up by the part they play, `DemoSeat`; mailboxes; website and email domains) in `server/providers/crm/demo-content/` (`zh-CN.ts`, `en-US.ts`, one `DemoContent` shape), and everything else shared in `demo.ts`, which holds no text of its own. Change both content files together. Its texts are long on purpose, so the pages show how long text reads: companies' introductions (which their deals' descriptions reuse) and leads' original messages run to several paragraphs, as do the leads' company introductions and every street address in the particulars, the tasks' description, results and the approvals; each of the eight companies has meeting notes on its timeline, some answered by a colleague (`companyNotes`); every converted lead carries the tasks done on it before its conversion and every invalid one the first contact that set it aside, each with its result (`convertedLeadTasks`, `invalidLeadTasks`, H World's `leadTask`); H World's lead is converted with its deal, so its earlier task links the lead, the company and the deal at once; the importer takes part in some of the colleagues' deal tasks, ahead and past (`internalParticipants`); every task done or cancelled is written as it happened, created open and then closed at its time with its result (the import's `saver`), so its timeline reads created, then completed with the result under that entry; and a comment's heading is a plain line above its text, never bold, a comment being what a person writes. The facts in the texts agree with the records' fields (employees, city, amounts). The administrator importing it is the demo's main account, a sales manager for its lifetime: they decide the sellers' and the manager's approvals (two waiting, one decided), and own, besides a share of the team's records, four projects at real companies (`projects-zh-CN.ts` and `projects-en-US.ts`, the same companies in both, their people and exchanges the demo's own): SF Express's new lead, H World's converted lead and deal at proposal with a discount out to the manager and tomorrow's review, GAC's deal in negotiation with approved payment terms and an overdue task, and New Hope won with an extension. The AI's work on them is written as its cards keep it (`crmHistory` rows of action `ai`, results typed against `Verdict`, `OpportunityVerdict`, `TaskPreparation` and `ApprovalReview`), so the import needs no model and the importer's inbox opens on five suggestions waiting and four handled; as the import ends the manager @-mentions them, replies to their comment and gives them a task, which the notification pass delivers. The importer also finds four finished conversations with Noco about those projects under the menu's Conversations (`chats` in the projects files): the Agents plugin creates each conversation and its messages are written into the plugin's `agConversationMessages` as its store writes them, the first question carrying the record it was asked about as its page context (H World's and GAC's deals, SF Express's lead), so the bar names it (`demoChats` in `server/routes/demo-services.ts`, `@temporary(nocobase-official)` until the plugin can import a finished conversation), recorded in the manifest as `chats` and deleted with the demo data; none is made without the plugin or before Noco exists. Removing the demo data (`resetDemo`) also deletes from everyone's inbox, once its records are gone, the in-app notifications whose route target names a CRM record that no longer exists, its own and any an earlier removal or a deletion left (`DemoInbox.removeOrphans` in `demo-services.ts`: the plugin's items read and checked against the CRM's tables, a former `/crm/accounts/` path against the companies', deleted through its `inAppNotificationStoreToken` store), and disables the demo accounts an import created (those of either language with a demo username at `@demo.example`; an existing account an import reused stays as it was), and importing again enables them and resets their passwords: they are disabled rather than deleted because the users plugin keeps a deleted account's address and username, so a deleted demo company could not be created again.

While no demo dataset is imported, the application layout shows whoever may import one (the settings capability, told by `demoPresent` in the metadata) a banner under the header (`DemoBanner` in `client/components/crm/demo-banner.tsx`, an application change to `client/layouts/app-layout.tsx`) linking to the settings' Demo data tab (`/settings/demo-data`); it goes once the import is done, and dismissing it is remembered in the browser (`localStorage`). While an import runs, dismissed or not, it shows how far it has got instead, on the registry `Progress` primitive: `GET /demoData` reports `importing` with the percent the import this process runs has reached (checkpoints in `populate`, the mailboxes, which take longest, step by step; null when another process runs it, the bar then indeterminate), the banner asks every second while it does, and the settings card marks the import under way the moment it starts it; `demoPresent` is false while the marker is only an import's claim, and a claim ten minutes old that no process holds (a server stopped mid-import) counts as none, a new import claiming it again (`abandoned` in `demo.ts`).

The demo dataset brings mail of its own, so the mailbox, the records' Mail tabs, the timeline and the follow-ups can be tried without connecting a real mailbox. `server/providers/crm/demo-mail.ts` is a mail provider of the CRM's own, type and instance `crm-demo` (configured in the defaults of `server/config/mail.ts`; a deployment's `mail.providers.crm-demo.enabled: false` turns it and the demo mailboxes off), registered at boot through the Mail plugin's public `mailProviderRegistryToken` and labelled in the demo's language (`server/locales/crm-ai/demo-mail.ts`). The import writes about sixty conversations into the `crmSettings` row `demo-mail`: a thread for every open deal (two for the busiest), the won and lost deals around their closing, the working, nurtured and invalid leads (one bounced by the delivery system, `postmaster`), exchanges between colleagues, and in every mailbox a newsletter, an event invitation, release notes and an office notice that concern no record (`everyday` in `demo.ts`; the topics' text in `demo-content/mail-zh-CN.ts` and `mail-en-US.ts`, the same topics in both, with placeholders for the dataset's people, and their senders as `mailSenders`). `buildDemoMail` files them in Inbox, Sent and Trash, threaded by their headers, the last message received unread, an exchange between colleagues in both mailboxes, no customer's message newer than two days, so nothing starts a follow-up, and no mail with a new lead, which would turn it working, then connects a mailbox `<username>@demo.example` for the importer and each demo seat through `MailService.connectAccount` with a password only this process holds (`DEMO_MAIL_SECRET`), recorded as `mailAccounts` in the manifest; removing the demo data removes those mailboxes and the row. The plugin copies a mailbox's messages once, on its first sync, and keeps read, starred and moved state itself, so the provider reports no changes; a message sent from a demo mailbox is accepted and filed in Sent without being delivered, and one to any address outside the `.example` domains is refused. Since a newly connected mailbox's history arrives dated before the timeline's cursor, `MailTimeline` keeps per user the mailboxes whose first sync it has seen (`synced`, from `listSyncRuns`) and reads the whole 180 days again when one appears.

The demo's companies are real, well-known Chinese companies (both languages, the English under their official English names): their names, cities, industries, rough sizes and real websites are public facts, while the people, deals and what happened with them are invented and kept neutral (no competitor chosen, budget cut, funding, incident or other claim that could read as a fact about the company; a lost or churned one built its own system instead). The contacts' email addresses stay at `<domain>.example`, never a real company's. Each lead's industry and city are `leadPlaces` in `demo.ts`. One project company, GAC, carries a real, neutral company signal with its real sources and date (`project.signals`; no model runs); removing the demo deletes its companies' signals. See [crm-signals.md](crm-signals.md).
