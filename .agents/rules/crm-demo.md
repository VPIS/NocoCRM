# The demo dataset

Read this before changing the demo dataset, its content files, its mail, its WhatsApp conversations, its import and clearing through the demo mode plugin, the demo's simulated AI and read-only exceptions, or the demo banner.

The demo dataset is the CRM's dataset for the demo mode plugin `@nocobase/app-plugin-demo-mode` (its Skill `nocobase-app-plugin-demo-mode` is synchronized into `.agents/skills/`; read it before changing the integration). The plugin owns the lifecycle: claiming an import, its progress, rolling a failed one back, recovering one cut off, clearing, resetting, the demo accounts and their roles, and the simulated `Demo model`; the CRM owns what the data says and how it is written and taken back. `server/providers/crm/demo-data.ts` registers the dataset (`crm`, languages `zh-CN` and `en-US`, time zones Asia/Shanghai and America/New_York) on `demoDataToken` at boot; its steps are `records` (`importDemo` in `server/providers/crm/demo.ts`, the whole story, which reports its own checkpoints through `context.progress`) and `models`. The routes, the page's hook and the command are the plugin's: `GET /api/demoMode/data` and `POST /api/demoMode/data/import|clear|reset` (`settings:demoMode.data`, which the CRM's administrator holds as root), `useDemoData()` on the settings' Demo data tab (`/settings/demo-data`, `client/pages/settings/demo-data.tsx`), and `pnpm nocobase demo-mode data status|import|clear|reset` (registered in `cli/plugins.ts`). The plugin's data routes exist only in a deployment running in demo mode (`NOCOBASE_DEMO_MODE` `read-only` or `observe`, the `demoMode` section of `server/config/demo-mode.ts`, off by default); elsewhere the tab says so and the banner stays away. The plugin is vendored until it is published: its build is copied into `app-plugins/demo-mode` by `node scripts/utils/sync-demo-mode-plugin.mjs <nocobase checkout>` (after `pnpm build` in its package), linked from `package.json`, and copied into `dist` by the build hook `scripts/utils/copy-demo-mode-plugin.mjs`. It comes in Chinese and English, chosen on import: the same story, told with people and companies of that language: its text and everything derived from a name (the demo accounts, each language's own, looked up by the part they play, `DemoSeat`; mailboxes; website and email domains) in `server/providers/crm/demo-content/` (`zh-CN.ts`, `en-US.ts`, one `DemoContent` shape), and everything else shared in `demo.ts`, which holds no text of its own. Change both content files together. Its texts are long on purpose, so the pages show how long text reads: companies' introductions (which their deals' descriptions reuse) and leads' original messages run to several paragraphs, as do the leads' company introductions and every street address in the particulars, the tasks' description, results and the approvals; each of the eight companies has meeting notes on its timeline, some answered by a colleague (`companyNotes`); every converted lead carries the tasks done on it before its conversion and every invalid one the first contact that set it aside, each with its result (`convertedLeadTasks`, `invalidLeadTasks`, H World's `leadTask`); H World's lead is converted with its deal, so its earlier task links the lead, the company and the deal at once; the importer takes part in some of the colleagues' deal tasks, ahead and past (`internalParticipants`); every task done or cancelled is written as it happened, created open and then closed at its time with its result (the import's `saver`), so its timeline reads created, then completed with the result under that entry; and a comment's heading is a plain line above its text, never bold, a comment being what a person writes. The facts in the texts agree with the records' fields (employees, city, amounts). Everything is dated from the import's clock (`context.clock`: its start and the dataset's time zone), as days before or after today there. The administrator importing it is the demo's main account, a sales manager for its lifetime (`context.assignPermissionSet`, unless they hold the set already; taken back on clear): they decide the sellers' and the manager's approvals (two waiting, one decided), and own, besides a share of the team's records, four projects at real companies (`projects-zh-CN.ts` and `projects-en-US.ts`, the same companies in both, their people and exchanges the demo's own): SF Express's new lead, H World's converted lead and deal at proposal with a discount out to the manager and tomorrow's review, GAC's deal in negotiation with approved payment terms and an overdue task, and New Hope won with an extension. The AI's work on them is written as its cards keep it (`crmHistory` rows of action `ai`, results typed against `Verdict`, `OpportunityVerdict`, `TaskPreparation` and `ApprovalReview`), so the import needs no model and the importer's inbox opens on five suggestions waiting and four handled; as the import ends the manager @-mentions them, replies to their comment and gives them a task, which the notification pass delivers. The importer also finds four finished conversations with the assistant about those projects under the menu's Conversations (`chats` in the projects files): the Agents plugin creates each conversation and its messages are written into the plugin's `agConversationMessages` as its store writes them, the first question carrying the record it was asked about as its page context (H World's and GAC's deals, SF Express's lead), so the bar names it (`demoChats` in `server/providers/crm/demo-services.ts`, `@temporary(nocobase-official)` until the plugin can import a finished conversation); none is made without the plugin or before the assistant exists.

The import's own record is the `manifest` in the plugin's dataset data (`DemoManifest` in `demo.ts`): the records it created, the demo mailboxes, the conversations, the WhatsApp numbers and the business settings it changed, written at every checkpoint together with `context.track` of the new records, outside any transaction (on SQLite the plugin's writes would wait for the connection a CRM transaction holds). The accounts go through `context.createAccount` with the CRM's role scope (`roleScopes: { app: [roleKey(role)] }`, which the users plugin requires on create): the two sales sets, which keep no last holder, never root, whose last holder is protected. The dataset sets `keepAccounts`: the users plugin keeps a deleted account's username and email reserved, so the demo's accounts are disabled on clear and enabled again, their password and name reset, on the next import; an existing account the plugin does not own aborts the import. Clearing (the plugin calls the dataset's `clear`, `clearDemo` in `demo.ts`, also after a failed step and for an import cut off) puts the CRM's agents' models back, removes the demo mailboxes and the conversations, deletes the manifest's records with their history, queued messages, the companies' signals and checks, the approvals' lifecycle log and the WhatsApp data, deletes from everyone's inbox, once its records are gone, the in-app notifications whose route target names a CRM record that no longer exists, its own and any an earlier removal or a deletion left (`DemoInbox.removeOrphans` in `demo-services.ts`: the plugin's items read and checked against the CRM's tables, a former `/crm/accounts/` path against the companies', deleted through its `inAppNotificationStoreToken` store), removes the demo mail row and puts the business settings back; the plugin then disables the accounts and drops their assignments, and removes the importer's sales manager assignment and the demo model service. Only what the import made goes: records people created by hand stay. There is no `clearAll` (a whole-CRM wipe): clearing is always scoped to the import.

The import also sets the business settings the dataset is written for (`demoSettings` in `demo.ts`): the currency to CNY, since every amount in it is in yuan; the business time zone to Asia/Shanghai for Chinese and America/New_York for English; and the AI working language to the dataset's. It sets only a setting still at its shipped default (`defaultSettings`), so an administrator's own choice stands, and records the values it replaced in the manifest first; a failed import and a clear put back each one that still holds the demo's value. Scheduled task titles are written in the AI language at boot, so they follow a change only after a restart.

Nothing about the demo reaches a real provider. While the plugin holds the CRM's dataset row (`crmDemoScope` in `server/providers/crm/demo-scope.ts`, in any state: importing, ready, clearing or failed), the CRM's own model calls (`generateQuietly` in `ai-model.ts`: follow-ups, reviews, preparations, captures, merges, write-ups) answer in process from the demo model (`models.complete`, tagged with the work), whatever model a work's agent names; the company signals' web search answers nothing (`signalsUnavailable`, and a lead's research is `unavailable`); and the daily passes (lead and deal signals, task preparation, company signals) are skipped (`skipped`, reason `demo-data`), so nothing rewrites the demo's curated AI work, while asking for one on a record still works. The `models` step points the CRM's own agents (the assistant, each work's agent, the Background AI) at the demo model alone, recording what each listed, so the assistant's conversations go through the Agents gateway to the plugin's simulated model; an agent an administrator made keeps its own models. The demo model's answers are `demoAnswers()` in `server/providers/crm/demo-answers.ts`: an approval's review, a lead's and a deal's follow-up and a task's preparation in the JSON each card parses, captures and merge proposals the smallest object their schema accepts, and the assistant first running `crm dashboard` through its shell and then telling the reader's tasks and approvals from what it printed, each in the language of the material and saying it is simulated. The CRM runs no Runner tasks of its own, so there is no Runner simulation. Mail, WhatsApp and files stay in process as described below.

In a read-only demo (`NOCOBASE_DEMO_MODE=read-only`) every change a request makes is refused, the lifecycle excepted. The workspace's best-effort write of the reader's language (`rememberLocale`) is swallowed. What an administrator may open for visitors is registered in `server/providers/crm/demo-writes.ts` and offered on the Demo data tab, rules starting empty: `crm.aiWork` (`crm_history` insert and update, scope `demo-records`: a record the plugin tracked for the dataset), wrapped by `demoWrite` around asking the AI about a lead, a deal, an approval or a task (`server/routes/operations.ts`); a record outside the demo is refused with `DEMO_SCOPE_DENIED`. The assistant's conversations write the Agents plugin's tables (`ag_conversations`, `ag_conversation_messages`, `ag_runs`, `ag_run_inputs`, `ag_agents`), which the plugin refuses to expose as a capability; a deployment that wants visitors to talk to the assistant in read-only mode lists them in `NOCOBASE_DEMO_ALLOW_TABLES`, or runs the demo in `observe` mode.

While no demo dataset is imported in a demo deployment, the application layout shows whoever may import one (the settings capability) a banner under the header (`DemoBanner` in `client/components/crm/demo-banner.tsx`, an application change to `client/layouts/app-layout.tsx`) linking to the settings' Demo data tab; it reads the plugin's public `GET /api/demoMode/status` first and asks for the dataset only in a demo deployment, goes once the import is done, and dismissing it is remembered in the browser (`localStorage`). While an import runs, dismissed or not, it shows how far it has got instead, on the registry `Progress` primitive, from the plugin's status, which `useDemoData` asks every second while it runs.

The demo dataset brings mail of its own, so the mailbox, the records' Mail tabs, the timeline and the follow-ups can be tried without connecting a real mailbox. `server/providers/crm/demo-mail.ts` is a mail provider of the CRM's own, type and instance `crm-demo` (configured in the defaults of `server/config/mail.ts`; a deployment's `mail.providers.crm-demo.enabled: false` turns it and the demo mailboxes off), registered at boot through the Mail plugin's public `mailProviderRegistryToken` and labelled in the demo's language (`server/locales/crm-ai/demo-mail.ts`). The import writes about sixty conversations into the `crmSettings` row `demo-mail`: a thread for every open deal (two for the busiest), the won and lost deals around their closing, the working, nurtured and invalid leads (one bounced by the delivery system, `postmaster`), exchanges between colleagues, and in every mailbox a newsletter, an event invitation, release notes and an office notice that concern no record (`everyday` in `demo.ts`; the topics' text in `demo-content/mail-zh-CN.ts` and `mail-en-US.ts`, the same topics in both, with placeholders for the dataset's people, and their senders as `mailSenders`). `buildDemoMail` files them in Inbox, Sent and Trash, threaded by their headers, the last message received unread, an exchange between colleagues in both mailboxes, no customer's message newer than two days, so nothing starts a follow-up, and no mail with a new lead, which would turn it working, then connects a mailbox `<username>@demo.example` for the importer and each demo seat through `MailService.connectAccount` with a password only this process holds (`DEMO_MAIL_SECRET`), recorded as `mailAccounts` in the manifest; clearing the demo data removes those mailboxes and the row. The plugin copies a mailbox's messages once, on its first sync, and keeps read, starred and moved state itself, so the provider reports no changes; a message sent from a demo mailbox is accepted and filed in Sent without being delivered, and one to any address outside the `.example` domains is refused. Since a newly connected mailbox's history arrives dated before the timeline's cursor, `MailTimeline` keeps per user the mailboxes whose first sync it has seen (`synced`, from `listSyncRuns`) and reads the whole 180 days again when one appears.

The demo dataset brings two WhatsApp numbers too (`server/providers/crm/demo-whatsapp.ts`), so the WhatsApp page with its number filter, the records' WhatsApp tabs and their timeline lines can be tried without a Meta account. The import writes two `crmWaNumbers` rows of mode `demo` (`demoNumbers`: the sales line `+1 555 010 0199` and the line abroad `+44 7700 900123`, both from ranges kept for fiction, their business names the content's `displayNames`) and eight conversations as the webhook would have kept them (`demoWhatsAppSchedule`, each naming its number, the texts in `demo-content/whatsapp-zh-CN.ts` and `whatsapp-en-US.ts`, message for message; `tests/logic/demo-whatsapp.test.ts` checks they agree): on the sales line Shanghai Electric's and United Family Healthcare's decision makers, the working lead from Tongwei, and H World's IT director and its evaluator (Cheng Hao, 16 messages on the POC's tests), so H World's company and deal hold two people's messages, linked by their numbers (`+86` and the record's phone), with a line a stretch of conversation on the records they reach (`WhatsAppService.rewriteTimeline`), and a job seeker set aside; on the line abroad a stranger known by a username alone and an integrator, nobody's yet. H World's deal's waiting follow-up is one a customer's WhatsApp reply started (`customerReplied`, last night just after Chen Lu's messages), its brief, prep and reasons drawn from them (the SLA he asked for, the remote presentation, procurement comparing prices), so the AI's use of WhatsApp shows on a card. Four customers' messages carry a picture or a file, their text the caption (`media` in the schedule, naming the content's `files`): Shanghai Electric's photo of its paper inspection sheet and its production reports' sample (a CSV) before the trial import, H World's load test report as a screenshot, and the job seeker's CV (a PDF). Each language has its own (`demo-content/whatsapp-files-zh-CN.ts`, `whatsapp-files-en-US.ts`), the bytes kept there as base64 so the import needs no network, generated with headless Chrome from HTML with made-up people and figures; the import stores them with the CRM's files as a downloaded one is (`WhatsAppService.storeFile`), and the file sweep removes them once a removal has deleted their messages. Three conversations are long, so the page's paging and a record's long history can be tried: Shanghai Electric's (55 messages over six weeks, past the 50 a conversation first shows, so Load earlier appears), H World's (33 over a month, the POC to the quote) and the integrator's (24 over two weeks); the others are a few messages each. The team's replies are origin `businessApp`, as made in the WhatsApp Business app, since the CRM does not send yet. A conversation awaits a reply as the webhook would count it (`awaitingReply`: the customer's messages since the team's last reply), five of them, nine messages in all; demo numbers are not `api`, so opening one does not clear it. No message is newer than yesterday and none sets `followUpDueAt`, so nothing starts a follow-up. The numbers are recorded as `whatsapp` in the manifest (version 8, `numberIds`; an import from before the second number has `numberId`, which removal still reads) before anything is written; removal deletes them with their conversations, messages, customers and their lines (`removeDemoWhatsApp`), and unlinks any other customer someone linked to a demo lead or contact. The demo numbers are never the company's: `WhatsAppService.numbers()` leaves mode `demo` out, and the workspace's `whatsapp.configured` is `available()`, a connected number or a demo one.

The demo's companies are real, well-known Chinese companies (both languages, the English under their official English names): their names, cities, industries, rough sizes and real websites are public facts, while the people, deals and what happened with them are invented and kept neutral (no competitor chosen, budget cut, funding, incident or other claim that could read as a fact about the company; a lost or churned one built its own system instead). The contacts' email addresses stay at `<domain>.example`, never a real company's. Each lead's industry and city are `leadPlaces` in `demo.ts`. One project company, GAC, carries a real, neutral company signal with its real sources and date (`project.signals`; no model runs); removing the demo deletes its companies' signals. See [crm-signals.md](crm-signals.md).

LinkedIn addresses in the dataset: a company's is its real LinkedIn page (`DemoCompany.linkedin`) only where a search showed the page as that company's own, left out otherwise rather than guessed (Intime, Guangzhou Restaurant and XJTLU have none); a person's, since the people are made up, is `demoProfile(mailbox)` in `server/providers/crm/demo.ts`, `https://www.linkedin.com/in/nococrm-demo-<mailbox>`, an address no real profile has, as their email is at a `.example` domain: every primary contact and the other contact at every other company, the importer's projects' primary contacts, and the leads that came by exhibition, outreach or a partner.
